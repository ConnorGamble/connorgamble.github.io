# D - Describe

Easy Deliver Co. is a cosy delivery game, dressed up in PS1 style graphics and a lot of charm. 
The core gameplay loop sees you play as a cat, the new delivery driver in town. (alright he's got no thumbs to drive but you've probably palmed the steering wheel before, don't think too much about it)
You pickup a parcel at point A, load it up in your trusty truck and deliver it to point B. On the way, you'll be fighting bad weather and your own driving skills due to the physics. Below the surface, and closer to the end of the game, it hides a much more harrowing reality for those living in this snowy town.

I came into it like the storefront pitches it, just a cosy game just about driving around delivering packages, the sort of experience where you can just switch your brain off for a few hours and follow the road. Turns out all that was in this package was horror...
# U - Unpack
## Moment to moment gameplay
It's worth mentioning upfront that there is no combat in this game. The main elements of challenge comes from the weather and road conditions. (Or, offroad conditions, in the case of some parts of the game). So naturally, the main gameplay loop consists of driving.

From the get-go the driving is fun, it feels satisfying enough to keep you engaged which is not to be understated since you're doing it for a good majority of the game. The physics of driving the deliver truck on it's own is well crafted, it feels heavy and clunky in a way which makes sense for a vehicle of it's size. (the header of this page shows the truck)

Add to this that the packages you pick up are **not** locked into the back. That's right when you put a package into the back of your truck it's able to move. It can slide around. It can tip over. It can balance on the guardrail as you take a corner a little too fast causing you to lose seven out of the ten pizzas you were meant to be delivering. That's definitely not based on a true story, I am a responsible delivery driver after all.

The added complexity of the packages challenges you to make decisions and weigh up the pros and cons of each individual package you're delivering. 
If you have a single large box, you can probably drive a little faster and take sharper turns because you know the guardrails have your back. 
On the other hand if you have some potted plants to deliver, they are smaller so you might be able to take a shortcut over some bumpier snowy hills launching the plant you're meant to be delivering into space. Again, definitely didn't happen to me. 
There is a good amount of diversity in packages too, from your standard boxed parcels to crates of beer (each of which are effected by physics individually) to potted plants, bags of soil. You never really know what's being picked up although you can take a good guess based on where you're picking it up from. 

There are various stores around each of the three maps which sell different things, from tea and coffee to duct tape and bird seed. As you'd imagine, they all have a use. Duct tape can be used to tape down the parcel which you're delivering. (wish I- sorry, wish *they* knew that before losing seven pizzas). Bird seed is for... well... feeding the birds. Who for some reason once they have finished eating will throw a couple of dollars your way. Not sure why but I didn't say no to getting some money back! 

Earning money from deliveries is generally more lucrative the further you need to deliver but it's always a bit of a balancing act. Further to travel means more fuel and that stuff is expensive. (not by real life standards, I wish it only cost me £50 to fill up!)

## World and Progression
There are three maps total, each of these have the same stores dotted around them as well as a single petrol (or gas if you're American) station around which you can see from the map you are given. They are clearly designed for you to progress with your truck so that you can take on less accommodating travel.

The first introductory area, called Mountain Town, doesn't have too much in the way of danger. Mostly clear roads with some larger patches of snow here and there which you'll need to avoid in the first hour or two of the game. 

The second has much more snow, aptly called Snowy Peaks where you're first introduced to the snow tires. Allowing your truck to have improved grip and go through patches of rougher snow, helpful across the rest of the game. 

The third, Fishing Town, has a massive ice lake in the middle which if you drive on, will start to crack. So you need to make sure to get to wherever you're going before it catches up to you. 

It's worth mentioning that although the game presents you with these maps, it does **not** show you where you are within them. That means you need to figure out where you are based on the road layout, nearby buildings and elevation. I personally loved this, it really helps you get to know the areas. This is also lore friendly because it states through some of the in game mail that:

> "Our best delivery drivers are those who have learned the layout of their assigned profit sector. As a result, we will not be providing any navigational aid."

All of which is provided through a handy `easy_os` interface.

## Presentation

### Visuals
The UI is presented in a stylised terminal/linux like operating system. When driving, the only things visible are: 
- Your current money
- The fuel level of your truck 
- The time
- The energy level of your character

Outside of the UI, as mentioned the PS1 era graphics really play well into the snow filled world, limiting view distance in most cases but not feeling like a copout, more just like a snowstorm is rolling in. The models and textures all mould together really well, nothing feels particularly out of place when put next to each other.

### Audio
Excluding the music, the audio is serviceable for it's function. The cars engine doesn't really change much, the wind and overhead train do the job to fit the scene. 
What really astounds me is how well the music fits in. Depending on the track, it can really enhance speeding down a road, as fast as you can watching your cargo wobble. Other tracks accommodate for a slower paced, almost Sunday drive levels of relaxation. 

The big thing I have with the music though is how _empty_ the world feels when it doesn't play. I don't mean that in a negative way either.

You can be in the warm confines of your truck cabin, listening to some relaxing jazz as the snow rages outside. You pull up to your pickup spot and exit the car. 

The vibe switches completely. 

Now you can hear is the overwhelming sound of the wind raging whilst the song you were just listening to plays quietly muffled from your car. Are you move further away from your truck, it gets quieter and quieter until you can only hear the howl of the wind. 

It's phenomenal. 

The more you talk to the characters too, the more their voices start to change. Initially they are just like Animal Crossing characters, with those higher pitched voice pips which are used to indicate expression. (sad, angry, happy etc). Over time though, they start to break down, they become deeper, more disgruntled, like a machine coming to a halt...

And it fits perfectly, given it's main story hook!

# C - Critique
## Strengths
- Fantastic visual style
- Satisfying driving physics
- Maps are well designed, easy enough to traverse
- The survival mechanics of energy and fuel consumption felt pressing, but never overbearing
- The story twist
- The length (it's not particularly long)

# K - Keepers
So I've mentioned it a couple a times, the big twist. So **spoiler alert**, I'd recommend that if so far it sounds like something which is up your alley then go and experience it now before spoiling yourself.

<details>
<summary>Click to expand spoilers</summary>

Turns out, all of the townsfolk are all robots. Left to rot because the previous maintenance bot, Seb, was shutdown. He was shutdown due to going "rogue" by quitting his job and wanting to spend time with MK, one of the other robots in town.

Initially, all the townsfolk mistake you for Seb, which is easy to brush off initially but as time goes on each one starts to break down. Instead of talking with lines of dialog they will start to spew error codes. Their audio voice pips become darker and more menacing.
</details>

The game presents you with three options. A good, a bad and a neutral ending.

<details>
<summary>The bad ending</summary>
The bad ending is probably the most harrowing. You shutdown the servers they all connect to so they all stand there, completely lifeless. Power to the entire town is also gone so there's no more streetlights, no more deliveries.

The empty, dark streets, the lifeless shells standing staring at the front door when you go into their stores, it made an already somewhat eerie environment just that much creepier.

 It does however mean you can go on a wicked shoplifting spree!
</details>


<details>
<summary>The neutral ending</summary>
The neutral ending involves you just doing a reset of the servers. Essentially making it so like all of the townsfolk had never met you, bar MK who remembers you. This ultimately doesn't really do much, just extends the cycle of neglect which the townsfolk have been going through.
</details>


<details>
<summary>The good ending</summary>
The good ending involves you purchasing a recovery disk which you can use to fix all of the townsfolk and have them remember you. Easily the most satisfying out of them all.
</details>

The bad ending is easily the thing which will stick with me the most about this game, the atmosphere cannot be matched.
# TL;DR
Easy Deliver Co. on the surface is a relaxing delivery game which doesn't outstay it's welcome. Clocking in around 6 hours or so for the 100% achievements, it's short and to the point.
The story, although not particularly complex or emotionally intense, still provides enough satisfaction that I'd recommend experiencing it yourself, even if it's just for an afternoon.
