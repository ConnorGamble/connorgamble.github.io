Citizen Sleeper

A Cyberpunk TTRPG with an incredible atmosphere

January 2, 2026 · 6 min read

D - Describe

What is it?

Citizen Sleeper is a Cyberpunk RPG set on a ruined space station. You play as a Sleeper, which is a human consciousness in a robot body. You're just trying to make your way in the world whilst being hunted by the corporation who wants that robot body back.

The core gameplay loop consists of "cycles" which are basically days. Each cycle you get a new set of dice to spend doing actions throughout the day. These dice change the outcome of the action you choose to do. For instance, if you're low on money you can go work a shift at the local bar. You roll a dice to see how well it goes. Good roll? You get some nice tips! Bad roll? You end up hurting yourself.

Expectations

I went into it knowing that rolling dice was a core mechanic, similar to that of Disco Elysium which I've played some of but not finished. With that high expectation in mind, let's unpack a little bit about the experience.

U - Unpack

Story

The story is decent, it delivers a coherent narrative without just exposition dumping and overwhelming the player. (or reader is probably more accurate) It explores the tropes of a Cyberpunk theme in a moderate way where it doesn't outstay it's welcome. Naturally with "punk" being part of the genre "Cyberpunk" there are some anti-capitalism themes but it doesn't dwell on them and it doesn't outstay it's welcome. Citizen Sleeper is focusing more on the sense of finding a place to belong, found family and the struggle to just survive day to day. It has a few standout moments with some of my favourite posing some question that prompt a deeper thought around your perception of what makes someone human.

My biggest gripe with it though is that it never really feels like my choices have much impact until the "choose ending" time near the end of stories. The dialogue choices you make through the game don't really reflect your class at all or have any impact on what the characters say or do, minus the next line or two which was slightly disappointing.

Dice Rolling

As mentioned, dice rolling is a pretty big mechanic which dictates most of the actions which you do in the game. When starting you pick a class, which in the beginning does have an impact on what you can do. For instance working in a shipyard if you pick the Extractor class, you're a bit more stocky so you likely end up hauling boxes around where they need to be. But if you picked machinist then you might be doing system repairs etc.

The mid-late game though is where this choice doesn't really matter. See as you complete story beats, you get upgrade points and you can spend them to enhance skills for better roll modifiers or other perks. You get to a point where you've maxed all the stats though and all rolls you get have a +2 bonus. (which is the max) Combo this with another perk you get, where you can re-roll your dice once a day and, luck permitting, you can basically get a successful roll on anything you do. If this came with experience doing the actions then I could understand that you're learning the skills but you can just avoid a lot of the skill checks you're not very good at so it's almost like you just magically get better at the skills.

Exploration & World

The story is set in the Erlins Eye, an unruly space station on the edge of a star system. Originally controlled by Solheim (one of the big corporations) but now falling into disrepair with the people on it trying to hold it together as much as they can. It's got two layers though, the physical layer where you'll meet people, do actions and spend most of your time and then the data layer.

The physical layer has a solid style, the buildings and lights always present it like you're an eye in the sky of the city, travelling to go here there and everywhere. It's simple enough, there's not a lot of complexity but you will need a decent scroll wheel because you'll be moving up and down quite a bit. My only critique with this method is that there is a shattered part of the space station which requires you to click a button to get some help across. It's not really needed past being a barrier to the next zone. Remove that and just let the player scroll (after paying the fee you need to open it) and I'd be absolutely fine with the world traversal.

The data layer is used to extract data which can be sold on for profit but requires you to sacrifice the correct die. It's only a couple of nodes which just give items, not really much to mention but it serves it's purpose fittingly.

Presentation (Visuals, Audio, UI/UX)

Visuals

The 3D model of station itself is easy enough to understand, you're mostly looking at it from a sort of "eye in the sky" perspective. It feels like there's enough going on there. There's little ships flying around, lights on in the nightclub showing people partying. It serves it's purpose and really sets the scene.

The artwork is heavily stylised with each character having a unique look sprite which look fantastic. The sprites feel fitting for the characters too. The tech nerd stands leaning on some sort of machine whilst having a single glove on and some small bits of tech strapped to his chest harness. The mycologist stands in cleaner clothes, lab-like attire whilst clutching a sample mushroom.

Can't fault it at all.

Audio

The soundtrack is wonderful, each and every track feels carefully created to not steal the show, but help set the tone. Some are more intense, helping pile the pressure on during the games more high octane scenes. Others are designed to provide a calming soundscape to evoke the feeling you're talking to an old friend or giving you the space to let you wander off into your thoughts during the times of solitude.

UI/UX

The UI is clean, sharp and very easy to understand. It all fits together really well and does the job it needs to do efficiently and with style.

Technical Performance

Citizen Sleeper is a really simple game, it's not doing anything particularly complex but it's performance is flawless and I encountered no technical issues with it whatsoever which is very refreshing.

C - Critique

Strengths

  • THE MUSIC
  • The character sprites and UI look incredible
  • The mechanics are intuitive and engaging enough to break up the story
  • Some of the endings are real tear jerkers

Weaknesses

  • Some stories fall a little flat near the end of their arcs
  • It didn't really feel like most of the dialogue options had much weight
  • Just let me scroll once I've unlocked the docking man, it's so small but you cross over it enough!

K - Keepers

  • The dice systems work well, simple enough to easily grasp but not impactful enough to feel like if you don't roll perfectly then you'll screw up the entire playthrough.
  • Two songs in particular, Coalesce and Possible Futures are incredible, such wonderful composition gives a really strange mix of emotions.

TL;DR

A pretty decent visual novel, leans less onto TTRPG than it says on the tin but that's not to it's detriments. Not a perfect game by any means but a decent way to spend a bunch of time and kick off some existential questions.
